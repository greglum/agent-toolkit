## Useful patterns and working method

Use Landbook to compare like with like. A relevant industry and page type produce better questions than a feed chosen only for visual fashion. Record how the page establishes context, groups information, prioritizes actions, and supports credibility. Decide which of those relationships fits our product.

Pricing references deserve particular care. Equal cards can make unequal plans appear equivalent, long checkmark lists can obscure meaningful distinctions, and fine-print terms can be difficult to find. Design our page around actual eligibility, billing cadence, limits, cancellation, and the next action. The reference should improve the explanation rather than supply a decorative grid.

For working apps, borrow typography, alignment, and grouping selectively. Sparse marketing layouts can waste space or bury frequent actions in an operational workspace. A screenshot of a signup page also cannot explain validation, recovery, consent, or authentication. Use a flow-oriented source when those behaviors matter.

Suggested workflow:

1. Select a matching industry and page or section type.
2. Compare two references for information order and action hierarchy.
3. Follow the original site when responsive behavior or interaction matters.
4. Write an adapted brief using real product content and existing tokens.
5. Verify long labels, small screens, keyboard behavior, and all required states.

Mobile and historical views can inform the brief when legitimately available, but they remain observations of another product. They do not override our content needs or accessibility requirements.
