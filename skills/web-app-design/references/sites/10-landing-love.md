## Useful patterns and working method

Use the recording format to study sequence: when a key message becomes readable, how sections enter and settle, how imagery supports a claim, and whether an action remains easy to find. A still image can conceal delayed content and transitions that compete with reading. A recording exposes some of that timing, although it still omits input and assistive-technology behavior.

For public product pages, a quiet reveal or purposeful visual transition can help explain a concept. For repeated app tasks, borrow the timing principle with restraint: feedback should be prompt, states should remain legible, and animation should not postpone the next action. A portfolio's immersive pacing is rarely appropriate for filtering a table or editing a record.

Suggested workflow:

1. Choose a category that matches the page's goal rather than its desired effect.
2. Record the sequence and reading hierarchy in original words.
3. Identify which content remains understandable when motion is removed.
4. Follow the original site before relying on scrolling or interaction behavior.
5. Implement only the useful mechanism through existing components and verify its fallback.

When comparing references, ask whether motion carries information or merely introduces it. If the same explanation works with a stable image and clear copy, begin there. Any extra animation must earn its attention and runtime cost. Do not make a critical action depend on scrolling to a precise moment in a sequence.
