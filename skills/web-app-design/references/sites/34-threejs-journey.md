# 34. Three.js Journey

**Observed:** 2026-10-05. **Research depth:** explicit official search for course/FAQ/license; homepage, public curriculum/introduction, WebGPU/TSL offer, and general conditions. Public pages and some lesson text were accessible. Locked videos, member materials, and downloads were not accessed.

## Purpose and audience

Three.js Journey is Bruno Simon's structured training for browser 3D developers. The public site describes beginner-to-advanced coverage and lifetime access. The curriculum contains fundamentals, animation, cameras, resizing, textures, lighting, particles, shaders, optimization, and React Three Fiber lessons. Its strongest role is developer education for a real 3D requirement, not a shortcut to better everyday product UX. [Official course](https://threejs-journey.com/), [curriculum and introduction](https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/introduction)

The observed catalog distinguishes free lessons from previews and locked content. It also advertises a separate WebGPU and TSL course. Current headline durations and student counts are vendor marketing figures, not independent measures of quality or a promise that every technique matches an app's installed Three.js version. [Course structure](https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/introduction), [WebGPU/TSL course](https://threejs-journey.com/webgpu-tsl)

## Useful patterns and workflow

For product configuration, spatial interfaces, or graphics tools, use the curriculum to identify the specific knowledge gap: camera interaction, asset loading, lighting, custom shaders, or resource cost. A team can learn that topic and apply it to its own scene. For a marketing experience, the same techniques can support interactive objects or an art-directed scene. These are fit assessments, not recommendations to add 3D to every web app.

The public introduction explains starting and final exercise files, lesson materials, and ways to study. This supports a bounded workflow: understand the relevant concept, implement it in the existing project architecture, and validate it in the target app. A finished exercise is an educational reference; it is not evidence that a production feature handles loading failure, accessibility, or low-end devices. [Study workflow](https://threejs-journey.com/lessons/introduction)

## Access, dependencies, and rights

The observed homepage displayed a $120 lifetime offer, while some lesson pages displayed different course pricing, and the WebGPU/TSL offer showed a separate price. Treat exact pricing, bundles, taxes, and promotions as purchase-time facts requiring recheck. No purchase or enrollment is implied by UI work or this research. [Observed offer](https://threejs-journey.com/), [separate course](https://threejs-journey.com/webgpu-tsl)

General conditions reserve ownership of platform content and restrict reproducing or integrating it. They distinguish examples of code lines in training exercises from other content, but this is not a blanket asset or open-source license. Inspect the exact code example and any texture/model notices before adoption. Course access does not authorize republishing videos, lesson prose, or downloadable assets in the skill. [General conditions, Article 5](https://threejs-journey.com/general-conditions)

## Use in the design skill

Keep this as an optional learning reference. Do not require purchasing it, change providers, or rebuild a normal app around the course stack. Use official Three.js documentation for current API details and connect learning to a concrete product need.

**Analytical checks:** usable DOM controls around canvas, keyboard alternatives, loading/error states, responsive camera behavior, reduced motion, context loss, asset budget, and disposal. Recheck curriculum, access conditions, price, exercise/asset rights, and version compatibility when choosing training or adopting a technique.
