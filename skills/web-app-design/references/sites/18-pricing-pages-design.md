# 18. PricingPages.design

Observed: 2026-10-05. Depth: targeted web search, homepage filters, About, Legal, and a Chroma detail page. Public pages were readable without login. No billing interaction or conversion experiment was tested. This research does not verify displayed prices for purchasing decisions.

Research method: searched `site:pricingpages.design about pricing page examples license`, opened the official site, and followed the primary pages listed below.

## Purpose and useful evidence

[PricingPages.design](https://pricingpages.design/) is a focused gallery of pricing-page references. Its filters distinguish components such as tier cards, comparisons, monthly/annual switches, FAQs, contact sales, and usage calculators from patterns such as one-off payment, subscriptions, and content-heavy pages. This taxonomy is more useful than selecting a page by color alone.

The sampled [Chroma entry](https://pricingpages.design/sites/chroma-tsc8m) includes an addition date, industry, pricing-pattern tags, desktop and mobile references, and an original-site link. It also contains an unrelated “404s”/error-page heading. That appears to be a page-content inconsistency, so agents should evaluate the actual entry rather than trust every template heading.

The [Legal page](https://pricingpages.design/legal) explicitly warns that screenshot prices may be outdated and says featured designs belong to their original creators. The [About page](https://pricingpages.design/about) frames the collection as inspiration rather than permission to reproduce copyrighted material. Claims about conversion quality are curation language; no experiment data was exposed in the inspected pages.

## Patterns and application fit

Start from the real commercial model: what is charged, what is included, what varies, and what users must know before committing. Then find references with the same decision structure. A usage calculator serves a different purpose from a three-tier subscription grid; forcing either into the other can hide cost information.

For product UX, this gallery can inform a billing overview, upgrade comparison, or entitlement explanation, but it does not supply the billing logic. Show the selected cycle, total charged, renewal terms, limits, and any overage rule clearly. A yearly price expressed as a monthly equivalent must not obscure the amount collected today. Represent uncertainty explicitly when usage or custom enterprise terms affect the final amount.

Reference notes should compare feature grouping, the relationship between price and units, distinction between free and trial, and placement of explanatory details. Decide whether the user needs quick scanning or a detailed matrix. Avoid marking a tier “recommended” merely because a template has a highlighted card; recommendation should have a reason tied to user needs.

## Reuse, access, and integration

This is a reference gallery, with no verified reusable source package or broad asset license. The site's legal terms retain ownership with the creators and describe screenshots as educational/inspirational. Submission permission granted to the gallery is not a reuse grant to us. Public browsing showed no paid gate. A linked site's checkout, product assets, or code has separate terms. Browsing adds no app dependency.

## Skill use and checks

Route pricing and plan-comparison design here after collecting the actual billing contract. Ask the agent to extract hierarchy and information presentation, write original copy, and link each claim about price or entitlement to current product data rather than a gallery screenshot.

Analytical checks: comparison-table semantics, keyboard-operable cycle controls, accessible calculator outputs, stable prices during toggles, text explanations for color-coded tiers, and narrow-screen readability. These require local testing; the gallery does not certify them.

Recheck legal terms before asset reuse, original pricing before a purchase, and reference captures after a pricing redesign. Revisit the plan when business rules change.
