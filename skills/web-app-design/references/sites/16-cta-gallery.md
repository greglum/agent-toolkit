# 16. CTA.gallery

Observed: 2026-10-05. Depth: targeted web search, public homepage, About page, categories surfaced by search, and the full CTA writing tips page. Browsing was open. No conversion experiments, analytics, source-code review, or runtime accessibility tests were performed.

Research method: searched `site:cta.gallery forms buttons popups design`, opened the official site, and followed the primary pages listed below.

## Purpose and useful evidence

[CTA.gallery](https://www.cta.gallery/) organizes call-to-action inspiration into buttons, purchase actions, downloads, forms, modals/popups, navigation, newsletters, and pricing/subscription. It also exposes industry and display-mode selection. This is useful when a surface has an unclear next action or when a form and its surrounding offer need a stronger hierarchy.

The [About page](https://www.cta.gallery/about) identifies Salim Lunat of Webestica as its founder and frames the gallery around engagement and conversion. That positioning is promotional. The inspected pages did not show experiment methods, effect sizes, sample sizes, or conversion evidence for individual designs. The X post's “conversion-tested” wording is therefore unverified.

The [writing tips](https://www.cta.gallery/cta-tips) recommend specific action language, concise labels, truthful urgency, visible contrast, addressing objections, and testing alternatives. Those are useful hypotheses to evaluate against the actual user's goal; “above the fold” placement and first-person wording should not become universal rules for application workflows.

## Patterns and application fit

For marketing, compare the offer, action label, reassurance, and supporting copy as one unit. For a working app, an action should identify what will happen: save a draft, invite a member, export a file, or create a record. Visual prominence should follow task importance and consequences. A purchase CTA's persuasive language is often inappropriate for deleting data, granting access, or committing a payment.

An effective reference extraction records the primary action, the secondary alternative, the information needed before acting, and how much commitment the action entails. It should also say why the reference matches our problem. The mere presence of a large colorful button does not show that users understand its result.

For forms, plan labels, required fields, validation, pending state, retry, and success feedback before choosing a visual style. Preserve typed input when submission fails. A decorative popup should not interrupt the task unless there is a user-centered reason to open it. No gallery screenshot establishes those behaviors.

## Reuse, access, and integration

The homepage and About page explicitly state that screenshots remain copyrighted to their respective owners. Treat them as references, not distributable assets. The gallery links to Webestica templates and icon products, which have separate terms and may have separate prices. Their presence does not license an unrelated featured CTA. No reusable code license for the gallery's examples was verified. Browsing adds no app dependency.

## Skill use and checks

Route action hierarchy, onboarding signup, and public conversion sections here. Ask the agent to extract clarity, commitment, and placement principles, then write original copy tied to the app's real behavior. Do not repeat conversion claims without measured product evidence.

Analytical checks: contrast, visible keyboard focus, labels and inline errors, loading feedback without double submission, accessible modal focus, honest billing claims, and a clear way to decline a promotional interruption. These are implementation acceptance checks, not measured findings about this site.

Recheck when a selected original site changes, before buying a linked template, or when claims such as “free,” “cancel anytime,” or limited availability enter our copy.
