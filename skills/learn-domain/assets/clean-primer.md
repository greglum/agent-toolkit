# Lantern-moth silk: primer

Synthetic fixture. The domain, standards and sites are invented.

## Primer

### How it works

- Farms rear lantern moths on trays and harvest cocoons when the shells darken. [S1 §1; Reported]
- The cooperative grades each cocoon lot by reeling a sample under LSG-4, then pays farms
  by grade and weight. [S2 §3.1, S1 §6; Checked]

### Entities and processes

| Entity | Created when | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cocoon lot | A tray is harvested and logged at intake | [S1 §2.1; Checked] |
| Reel test | Intake draws a ten-cocoon sample from a lot | [S2 §3.1; Checked] |

### Metrics and traps

- Mean reel length of the sample sets the grade, so one short-reel sample can drop a lot a grade. [S2 §4.2; Checked]
- Moisture above 14 percent blocks grading under rule 7.3. [S3 rule 7.3; Checked]
- The MX-2 reads about one point high below 5 degrees Celsius. [S4 p. 9; Reported]
- Rework rate probably rises in wet months because moisture holds lots back. [S3 rule 7.3, S1 §5; Analysis]

### Newcomer mistakes

- Newcomers call a cocoon lot a `batch`, but the intake system uses that word for a truck load. [S1 §2.4; Checked]
- Whether grade D lots are ever re-reeled is unknown. [Q2; Open]
